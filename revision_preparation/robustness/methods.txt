Coverage robustness from original-reference DOI keys

The extension reads the corrected v1.2.0 SQLite snapshot and requires a complete reference-checked reproduction of analysis version v1.2.1. The 29 registered baseline tables retain their original definitions and registry. This extension creates ten additional tables in a fresh output directory.

The source unit is one immutable occurrence_id. Occurrences join catalogue units, working groups and source publication years many-to-one. The query reads the preserved reference string, source locations and workflow status. It reads no paper metadata or WoS record table. Workflow status is used only to describe selection after DOI extraction; it never determines membership in an analysis cohort.

One identity key is a DOI token extracted afresh from the preserved reference. The literal parser and a conservative punctuation-repair parser are the same functions used by the registered v1.2.1 raw-identity analysis. Punctuation repair normalizes Unicode dashes and joins whitespace immediately adjacent to DOI punctuation. An occurrence enters a DOI-key cohort when exactly one usable token remains. Entries with zero or several usable tokens are counted and excluded. A DOI token is an observed bibliographic identifier. This procedure does not independently verify its correspondence with the cited work.

The first analysis enumerates raw DOI recurrence in AR4–AR5 and AR5–AR6, both pooled and within WGI, WGII and WGIII. Repeated references establish one DOI-key membership per report or report/group. Retention divides shared keys by earlier keys. Reuse divides shared keys by later keys. Eligibility-adjusted reuse restricts later keys by the printed leading year. The primary rule uses the maximum observed known year for a later DOI key; missing-year entries do not change that maximum. A second rule requires a year for every occurrence and agreement among all observed years. Both rules are reported. Boundaries are 2006/2007/2008 for AR4–AR5 and 2012/2013/2014 for AR5–AR6. Common-vintage comparisons restrict both reports to 2000–2007 or 2000–2013, respectively. Each selected key must have at least one observed year, a minimum at or above the lower boundary and a maximum at or below the upper boundary.

The second analysis derives citation age from the leading year in the first 300 characters of each original reference. The parser accepts annual suffixes such as 2013a. Source-publication-year ages use an unambiguous year in the attached source record. Cutoff-origin ages use the documented AR5 and AR6 working-group literature-cutoff years retained in the baseline methods. No fallback origin is applied. Missing origins, missing cited years, future years and ages above sixty are enumerated. The included range is zero–sixty.

Occurrence weighting assigns each included entry weight one. Equal-DOI weighting assigns each included DOI key total weight one within its report or working group and divides that weight among its occurrences. A separate equal-working-group summary averages the normalized age distributions of WGI, WGII and WGIII. Each group receives weight one third. Cross-group DOI membership remains part of each group's distribution. This standardization fixes group weights and retains each group's observed raw-DOI selection.

The age tables provide inverse-ECDF quartiles, mean, population standard deviation and two defined recent-reference fractions. The fraction aged at most five includes six integer values, zero–five. The strict under-five fraction uses zero–four. Full weighted ECDFs are exported. Differences are AR6 minus AR5. There are no hypothesis-test p-values.

Composition intervals use 2,500 draws with seed 20261011. Catalogue units with selected DOI entries are resampled with replacement within each report and working group. Units remain in the draw even when their selected entries fail age inclusion. Independent report draws produce paired AR6-minus-AR5 differences. Equal-DOI weights are recalculated among the keys represented in every draw. Empty draws are recorded; an interval is supplied only when at least 95% of paired draws are nonempty. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles describe chapter-composition stability within this selected snapshot. They do not estimate uncertainty from extraction, identity errors or the unobserved climate-literature population.

Raw DOI presence supplies a different selection rule from WoS matching. It remains uneven across reports and working groups. Consequently these analyses condition on printed DOI availability. They do not equate source coverage, validate population linkage precision or identify the literature that IPCC could have cited. No new outlet result is computed because the stored outlet fields were enriched and have not been independently validated against the raw citation text. Existing outlet analyses remain separate.
