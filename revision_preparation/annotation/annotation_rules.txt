IPCC reference annotation protocol

Start the local workspace

Use Python 3.10 or later. No additional packages are required.
From this directory, run:

  python3 local_annotation_app.py

The app finds database/ipcc_litdb.sqlite when it is inside the repository.
For another location, pass the saved snapshot explicitly:

  python3 local_annotation_app.py --db /path/to/ipcc_litdb.sqlite

Open http://127.0.0.1:8765. The server binds to this machine only. All labels
save in work/. Original inputs and empty templates remain unchanged. The app
makes no network requests to external data services. Source PDF and DOI links
open only when selected. Stop the server with Ctrl+C.

AI-assisted suggestions

The ai_review/ package contains initial Codex proposals for the fixed sample.
The browser shows the proposal, evidence and open questions separately from
your annotations. Use as draft for this ID copies blank fields only. It keeps
review and duplicate-search completion pending and preserves existing labels.
Check the source entries, resolve open questions and enter your actual reviewer
code before marking the ID complete. A completed review attributed to Codex or
an AI reviewer is rejected by the browser and CSV validator. AI initial-review
counts are not human validation results.

Sample and units

The frozen current sample has 331 IDs: 319 probability IDs and 12 targeted
supplements. It contains 801 raw AR5/AR6 occurrences. The 33 probability strata
cover 46,715 distinct recorded IDs in the union of the matched AR5 and AR6 sets.
The 801 exported occurrences are AR5 (369) and AR6 (432). Other-report occurrences
of sampled IDs are outside the within-ID and metadata audit. The sampling seed is 20261011. Strata, source strings, keys and weights are fixed.
An occurrence is one saved bibliography entry. An ID can contain several entries.
No manual annotation existed when these empty templates were prepared.

Review an ID

1. Read every original entry shown for the ID. Open its source PDF and locate
   the bibliography entry. Compare candidate publisher, DOI or report records.
2. Assign a work-group letter to each occurrence. Use A for one study, B
   for another, and so on. Letters have meaning only within the current ID.
   When a raw entry clearly joins two distinct studies, record A | B and explain it.
   Verified publication versions of the same study share a letter.
3. Check preferred title, DOI, year, authors and journal. Record metadata errors
   separately from identity errors. Enter the affected fields and evidence.
4. Give the ID identity label and, where applicable, its shared-link judgment.
   Add reviewer initials and a short note or evidence URL. Set review_complete
   to yes only after every occurrence has a work group and metadata judgment.
5. Conduct the separate missed-merge search. Record the queries and results.
   Set its search status to complete only after the specified searches are done.

Identity labels

correct_merge: All reviewed occurrences under the ID represent one study under
the verified-version convention below. An ID with one original entry has no internal merge to contradict this
label. This label describes within-ID grouping; it does not establish that every
preferred field is correct or that the document has no duplicate ID elsewhere.

false_merge: The ID contains occurrences of at least two distinct studies under
the verified-version convention. Use distinct work groups. Identify the studies and explain whether the error
comes from record linkage or an extraction that joined bibliography entries.

unresolved: The available evidence does not establish the identity. Keep the ID
review incomplete. The estimator does not turn this label into a correct record.

Version convention authorized by the author on 21 October 2026: group a working
paper, preprint and journal article as one study after verifying their version
relationship. An explicit version-of-record link or a documented revision chain
can establish that relationship. A similar title or shared authors alone cannot.
Keep the source identifiers, cited version and publication dates in the notes.
The author also specified that citation age uses the verified year of the version
actually cited. Retain the final-publication year separately. A final-article year
substituted for an earlier cited working-paper year is a metadata error for that
occurrence, even when their study identity is correct. These proposals do not
change the frozen snapshot or the existing registered age results.

An online-first article and its final published version can be the same work.
Different publication-year labels alone do not establish a false merge. Compare
the DOI, authors, title, document version and publisher record. Treat separate
editions or substantively different reports as different documents. Record the
version decision in notes. A chronology flag is a cue for review, not a label.

Shared-link labels

The observed shared-matched set contains 1,657 IDs. Membership requires a
matched_doi or matched_title occurrence in each of AR5 and AR6.

same_work: At least one reviewed study occurs in matched entries in both
reports. Its work-group letter therefore appears on both sides. A false-merged
ID can still contain a real common study, so false_merge does not automatically
mean false_shared.

false_shared: The recorded ID is in both matched report sets, but the studies
represented by the AR5 matched entries and AR6 matched entries do not overlap.
Their work-group sets have no intersection.

not_shared: The ID is outside the original shared-matched set. Some occurrences
may exist in both reports while only one report has a recorded matched status.

unresolved: Cross-report identity remains uncertain. Keep review_complete blank
or no. The validation checks shared-link decisions against the work groups.

Occurrence metadata

correct: The checked preferred fields represent the cited document under the
document/version convention stated above. An allowed online/print year choice
is not automatically an error.

metadata_error: At least one preferred field is wrong. Identify doi, title,
year, journal, authors or other. Give notes or evidence. The weighted estimand
counts IDs with any erroneous reviewed occurrence. Occurrence counts describe
the reviewed entries and have a different denominator.

unresolved: The checked metadata remain uncertain. Do not mark the ID review
complete until this decision is resolved.

Missed-merge search

A missed merge is the same study, including verified publication versions,
assigned to another recorded ID. A within-ID
review alone cannot identify its absence. Search the raw DOI where present and
title variants, then author and publication year. Search the full saved database.
Inspect candidate raw entries and their source bibliographies. Preferred metadata
can themselves be wrong, so check the raw strings. Record useful query variants,
the search scope and candidate evidence in missed_merge_search_notes.

Use the Original raw reference search for raw DOI, title and author-year variants.
Preferred DOI/title/author searches supplement this original-text search. Their
search basis is displayed. Choose methods from raw_reference, raw_doi, title,
author_year and candidate_raw_entries. A complete search requires raw_reference,
at least raw_doi or title, plus author_year, and notes.
Record incomplete when a source, search or candidate check remains outstanding.

none_found: The completed protocol found no confirmed counterpart ID. This means
no missed merge was found in that audited search; it is not a guarantee of global
record-linkage recall.

missed_merge_found: Another ID represents the same study under the verified-version
convention. Record its positive
integer ID in missed_merge_candidate_ids, with | between several IDs. Include
candidate_raw_entries among completed methods and record the confirming evidence.
Do not include the current ID. Do not count several counterpart IDs as several
positive sampled IDs: the outcome is whether this audited ID has any counterpart.

unresolved: Candidate equivalence remains uncertain. Keep the search incomplete.

Labels and files

The browser saves only annotation fields. It exports ID and occurrence CSVs with
all original keys, source fields, selections and sampling weights retained.
Blank decisions remain blank. Work changes are recorded in work/change_log.jsonl.
An invalid or contradictory decision is reported by Check annotations. The app
does not replace the decision or repair an imported file.

Validate exported labels:

  python3 annotation_tools.py validate --id-labels work/id_annotations.csv \
    --occurrence-labels work/occurrence_annotations.csv

Generate results in a new output directory after annotation:

  python3 annotation_tools.py estimate --id-labels work/id_annotations.csv \
    --occurrence-labels work/occurrence_annotations.csv --output outputs/review_01

Missing files, missing keys, invalid labels, conflicting duplicates or altered
source/weight fields block estimates. Row order does not affect weighting.
Identical duplicate rows are counted once and reported. The default estimator is strictly closed until every one of the 331 ID reviews,
all 801 occurrence judgments and all required missed-merge searches are complete.
Incomplete labels produce only an integrity report and a nonzero CLI exit; no
rate or sensitivity files are written. Targeted cases remain separate descriptive
counts and never receive probability weights. Validation and the browser remain
usable while annotation is in progress.

Statistical method

For a binary ID outcome, the point total is sum_h N_h * k_h / n_h. The denominator
is the known ID population of the stated domain, not the number of raw entries.
Within-ID identity precision and false-merge and metadata-error rates refer to
the 46,715-ID union frame, based on the exported AR5/AR6 entries only. They do
not assess within-ID grouping of unexported other-report occurrences. Shared-ID outcomes refer to its 1,657-ID shared domain.

The design treats seeded hash ranking as pseudo-random simple random sampling
without replacement within the fixed strata. For each noncensus stratum, the
interval inverts both hypergeometric tails for the unknown finite-population
positive count. Bonferroni allocation gives simultaneous 95% stratum coverage.
Summing stratum bounds and dividing by the known domain population gives a
conservative 95% design interval. Census strata have exact bounds. Zero observed
errors in a noncensus stratum still permit a positive upper error bound. A
one-ID noncensus stratum has an exact interval even though its sample variance
cannot estimate a design standard error. The standard error uses the stratified
finite-population variance formula when estimable; it does not define the interval.

Missed-merge estimation measures the weighted share of audited IDs with a
confirmed counterpart under this search protocol. It does not estimate all
missing links, the number of unique duplicate pairs, or global linkage recall.

Recurrence sensitivity uses weighted shared-ID false-merge and false-shared
counts separately. It assumes each affected ID loses its shared-numerator
contribution and holds AR5 (13,503) and AR6 (34,869) membership denominators fixed.
The bounds transform the corresponding error-count interval. A false merge can
retain a genuine shared document, so the false-merge scenario is a loss scenario.
These calculations do not build a corrected crosswalk or corrected unique-work
counts. Metadata errors and confirmed missed merges have different mechanisms
and are not subtracted from recurrence as if they were false shared IDs.

Public delivery

The code, protocol, frozen sample and empty templates are portable. Keep work/
and outputs/ out of version control while annotating. Review any annotation
release separately. No analytical output is registered until actual labels pass
validation. The original 29 registered reference tables remain unchanged.
